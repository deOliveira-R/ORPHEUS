A name collision is settled by FORMAL FIT, not by who spent the word first:
the object whose formal definition the term of art matches (with the breadth and
depth of that term's mathematical properties) gets the name; the other object,
for which the name is one of several viable alternatives, cedes and is renamed.
When two objects are both perfect matches on different axes, the tiebreak is
which one has a STRONGER alternative name (that one moves to it).

**Why:** the user, 2026-09-26, on `HomogeneousProblem` ("whoever has a strong
formal name gets that name ... has to cede the strong name to the perfect
match"; the name must carry "a strong signal of what it is and the breadth and
depth of its mathematical properties").

**How to apply:** in a naming dispatch, D6(b)'s spent-word grep now produces a
CONTEST table (candidate objects × formal definition satisfied? × alternatives),
not a kill. Check the literature's internal method/result distinctions first
(Stetter: discretisation METHOD = rule, discretisation = result tuple) — the
direction of a spent word often decides who is the rule and who the result.
First applied: `scratch/posing_sequence/layer1_name_memo.md` (Discretization to
the layer-1 result, P1's `Mesh1D(geometry, discretization)` argument cedes;
"homogeneous" to the medium, source-free kind → `EigenProblem`). Related:
lessons.md D6(b).
