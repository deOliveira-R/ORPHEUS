## The ontology as it stands (consolidated 2026-09-26 from the rulings ledger; supersedes the morning's proposal, whose text is at `db9d8996` in git; the reasoning is in the sections below)

Three layers, each depending only on the one before it. Every statement here is RULED unless marked.

**Layer 1: the discretisation.** A method's discretisation of the source-free balance on a material mesh: the product of its per-axis discretisations (the mesh and the spatial scheme in space, the quadrature and the angular closure in angle, the group structure in energy; the user: *"Isn't the fact that we're doing these exactly what makes SNDiscretization earn its name?"*), holding the discrete spaces they induce (with their measures and metric, so `.H` and the Riesz legs are defined), the restriction map that projects a method-free source onto the unknowns (`project(source)`, owed), and the TERMS of the balance, each a real, timeless operator named by its physics (`streaming`, `collision`, `scattering`, `n2n`, `fission`, `boundary`, `time`) and each a function of the non-affine parameters it depends on. The physical point, the anchor, is where the balance is `L + C − S − N_2n − F − B` with the time term's coefficient 0. Layer 1 holds no question, no pencil, no source, no sweep machinery, and no "loss"/"production"/"gain". The materials are a field of the discretisation (has-a), not its base class.
- Names: `SNDiscretization`, `DiffusionDiscretization`, `InfiniteMediumDiscretization` (the 0-D method; the package `orpheus/homogeneous/` becomes `orpheus/infinite_medium/`), `CPDiscretization`, `MOCDiscretization` (with each method's campaign; Monte Carlo has no discretisation), and the Protocol `Discretization` (was `TransportMethod`, `transport/method.py:150`). `MaterialMesh` is unchanged.
- The carve's done-when: (a) no question or sweep member on a discretisation (a structural gate); (b) the terms are members, and at the anchor their signed sum equals today's `loss − production` and the k pencil built from them equals today's `problem.pencil`, both bit-identical; (c) `project(source)` passes an order-of-accuracy test.

**Layer 2: the problem (a question bound to a discretisation).** A question is method-free data, stored in the input layer with the reference specification; `numerics`' problem types bind it to a discretisation and never import it. Two kinds, told apart by whether a source is given ("homogeneous" goes to the medium, by the perfect-match ruling):
- `EigenProblem` over the question value `Eigen(parameter)`: where along the one-parameter family of balances `T(σ)` through the anchor is the balance singular? The parameter is a closed set: `TermCoefficient(channels)` (k: fission; c: every collision emission; α: the time term; an absorber density) or `GeometryExtent(which)` (the outer extent, uniform dilation). Whether the family is affine (a pencil) or not (a root over pencils) is DERIVED from the parameter AND the discretisation, by a per-scheme second-difference test (the outer extent is affine for SN diamond difference at a fixed cell count, rational for diffusion finite differences). The spectral map is derived from the parameter; `CriticalParameter` dissolves into `Eigen`. A mode selector says which pole: `Dominant`, `Nearest(τ)`, `Enclosed(region)` (never `Index(n)`). The adjoint needs no datum.
- `FixedSourceProblem` over `FixedSource(source, point)`: the response to a source that does not depend on the unknown (a contribution that does is an operator term, another question's answer, or a Strategy's splitting; a genuinely nonlinear dependence is a future third kind, the nonlinear feedback problem, not built). The point is the anchor (a fixed source, a subcritical multiplying source) or a point on a family (noise at `s = iω`, a Laplace variable); the field (ℝ or ℂ) is carried by the point's type, and complexification happens at the binding (`space.complexified`). The adjoint datum is a `Functional`, the `detector`. At a pole by construction (the k-normalised anchor: generalized perturbation theory, a static feedback source in a critical core) the question carries the solvability law `solvability(q) = ⟨ψ†, q⟩ = 0` and the CANONICAL gauge `⟨ψ†, M x⟩ = 0`; an inadmissible source grows secularly.
- A transient is an initial-value question at layer 2, realised as a chain of fixed-source questions at `s = 1/Δt` each re-anchored on the previous answer; the time grid and scheme are the QUESTION'S OWN DISCRETISATION (answer-changing, so not the Strategy's; of the question's axis, so not layer 1's).
- One question's answer can anchor or source another (perturbation sources `−δΣψ₀`, a transient's previous step); a Strategy never changes the question (no automatic "supervisor").

**The objects of layer 2.**
- The pencil of an affine eigen question is the family's first-order jet at the shift origin: `at(σ) = unshifted_balance + σ·balance_derivative`, where `unshifted_balance` is the balance at `σ = 0` (the carried-off point, NOT the anchor) and `balance_derivative` is `∂T/∂σ` (for k it is `−F`: negative; positivity belongs to the quotient). No physical accessor lives on a question (no `loss`, `fission`, `production`, `gain`); which terms sit on each side is read from `Eigen(parameter)`. `OperatorPencil` keeps its name (shared by both kinds of question).
- `pseudo_resolvent(σ) = at(σ)⁻¹·(−balance_derivative)` (Pazy, Yosida: it satisfies the resolvent identity and is a resolvent only when the derivative is injective), with `next_generation = pseudo_resolvent(0)` for k (`ρ = k`). "Propagator" stays with `(L + C)⁻¹`, "multiplication operator" with `M[f]`, "iteration operator" with layer 3's `M⁻¹N`.
- Both kinds use ONE object, the resolvent `R(σ) = at(σ)⁻¹`: a fixed-source question is a point value `R(σ₀)q`; an eigen question is the Laurent datum at a pole (the residue `ψ ⊗ ψ†`); higher harmonics are a contour integral; perturbation theory is the pole's derivative. `T(p)⁻¹` of a non-affine family is not a resolvent.
- Shifts: `at(σ)` is the only evaluation verb; `pencil.shifted(σ)` (law `shifted(σ).at(τ) == at(σ + τ)`); `OperatorPencil.jet(family, p0, h)` builds the local pencil of a non-affine family; `SpectralMap` becomes a composable Möbius group shared with the Strategy's `SpectralTransformation` (Wielandt, shift-invert, polynomial filters, contours, continuation), whose group law (fix the eigenvectors, invert the chart) is the question/Strategy gate. "Shift" names the scalar only. Owners: complexification, enlargement (delayed neutrons on `V ⊕ W`), the adjoint, the mode selector and the region or frequency set of a chain are the question's; charts and continuation paths to the asked endpoint are the Strategy's.
- Analysis quantities: a rate is the flux paired with the Riesz-LOWERED cross-section field, `R(φ) = ⟨♭Σ, φ⟩` with `♭ = space.riesz_lower()`; `Σ` is the summed field of a selectable CHANNEL SET under a counting mode (reactions, or emitted neutrons); the adjoint-weighted rate lowers `φ† ⊙ Σ`. `IntegratedReactionRate` and the four "production" spellings (#517) retire.
- `generator` is minted now: its perfect match is the time family's generator `−v·T⁻¹A`; the project's stage-2 "generators" cede (`Axis.generator` → `Axis.provenance`, `factory` admissible second); a group's generators keep theirs (qualified by their object).

**Layer 3: the solution.** A Strategy (evaluation points and paths that cannot change the answer; a partition per axis; the implicit/explicit labelling of the terms of `at(σ)`, derived from the terms' declared coupling pattern per axis (D1), closing-edge rank (D2) and sign structure (D3), with Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel the two extremes of the implicit set; a schedule; a lowering) and the Solution (the problem, the outcome, the strategy, the certificate, the record). Its sides are `implicit` and `explicit`, its quotient the `iteration_operator` `M⁻¹N`; at the physical point the layer-2 quotient and one splitting's iteration operator coincide (a pole against a rate), which is documented as the meeting point, not a shared naming family. A condensation that accelerates a solve is the Strategy's; one that produces a coarser layer-1 object for another question is a new Problem.

**Still to build, with its campaign** (not in P1): everything above except the question VALUES, which P1's step 7 stores in the reference specification.

## The goal, in the domain's terms

From the operators a method has posed on its space to a computed answer, name every step as an object in its right place, so that: the question asked (k, c, α, noise, a fixed source, a subcritical source) is data, never a code path; every legitimate way of computing an answer to one question (a shift, a resolvent, a splitting along any axis, Jacobi or Gauss–Seidel, a schedule, a lowering) is a value the machinery can derive or check from the operators' own declared structure; and no step carries data that belongs to another.

## The first-pass synthesis under attack `[R]` (the orchestrator, 2026-09-25; full text in `reference_cache.md` "P1, fifth exchange")

2. 3. Five kinds of object: the factors; one generic labelled piece set with `transfer` and `refine`; the pencil built from a labelling; a per-axis partition; the Strategy values composing them.
4. The specification names the carrier in data-layer vocabulary (`FISSION`, `EMISSION`); `CriticalParameter` keeps the non-affine parameters.

The converged answer to the user's attacks `[R]`:
1. **Attack (1) lands.** The Problem should end at its anchored term set: the signed operators `T_p` of one balance, and the physical point (the anchor, where `T = L + C − S − N_2n − F − B`; a term present in the data but absent at steady state, such as `1/v`, has coefficient 0 there), plus the source when one is posed. The pencil is not the Problem's last step; it is a view the QUESTION derives (`OperatorPencil.along(terms, carriers)`), and `hub.pencil` survives as the k question's view.
2. **Attack (2) lands, and the middle stage is the question itself.** A question is a curve through the anchor (which terms carry the parameter) plus what is asked of the resolvent on it: a pole (an eigenvalue), the value at a point (a fixed source, noise at ω, a Laplace variable), a path, a contour, or a root over a family of Problems (a non-affine critical parameter). It is a VALUE, which is why the reference specification can store it. Its laws: the family passes through the physical operator; the eigenvalue scale is derived from the carrier set; the adjoint of a pole needs no datum, of a source one detector; a rational family (delayed α) becomes affine on an enlarged Problem (neutrons plus precursors); one question's answer can anchor another (noise at the k-normalised point). The line between question and Strategy is a gate: a Strategy choice may not change the answer. This reverses two landed rulings: "α and noise are one Problem, two Strategies" (`consumers_step2_design.md` §3.5) and F11 (the fission-suppressed `at(0)` as a Strategy point): those points determine the answer, so they belong to the question.
3. **Attack (3) lands with one correction.** Triangularity is a property of a combination of operators under an order, never of one operator (the retired `creates_sweep_cycle` flag failed for this reason, `sweep_acyclicity.py:24-31`). Three declarations suffice: D1, the coupling pattern of each operator per axis (on the kernel, the scheme or the boundary law); D2, the rank of the closing edges per strongly connected component (close or split, report v2 §I.6); D3, the sign structure that lets a comparison theorem rank two splittings (Varga's regular splittings; the step scheme satisfies it on 384 of 384 probe configurations, diamond difference on 12 of 384). The lower and upper parts then follow from a partition and an order (generalising `SNBoundaryOperator.split`); Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel are not read off the explicit side, they are the two extremes of the implicit set (empty order and full topological order). `BlockRole` is a lossy seed of D1 (its join labels `C + B` as full although the sum is block-diagonal).
4. **The sequence:** MaterialMesh; the method's discretisation (spaces, per-axis partitions); the factors with their D1 to D3 declarations; the anchored term set (the Problem's end); the question (the family, the pencil as a view, the posing); the Strategy recipe; instantiation at the question's points; lowering and drivers; the Solution. ## The user's cooperative report (2026-09-26): `.claude/plans/posing_sequence_attack.md`, reasoned against this plan `[R]`

The report ("Middle-Layer Functional Ontology") proposes three layers: a physical basis (the operators T, S, F and V⁻¹, the measures, Gram matrices and Riesz maps; real-valued, timeless and source-free), a middle layer (complexification, the Laplace variable s, a physical parameter p, the generalized posing, automatic differentiation) and a solution trajectory (partition, splitting, Petrov–Galerkin condensation, lowering to XLA). It organises every system by a 2×2 table: at a pole or off it, with or without a source.

**What it adds, and how this plan changes** (for the user's ruling):
1. **The Problem is source-free.** The review's converged answer ended the Problem at "the anchored term set, plus the source when one is posed". The report is right that the source belongs to the question: one Problem admits many sources (superposition), and some sources are made from another question's answer (a perturbation source `−δΣ ψ₀`, a detector response). So the Problem ends at the real, timeless, source-free term set with its spaces, measures and metric; a source (or, adjoint, a detector) is part of the question. This matches the reference specification's question types, which already carry the source (`FixedSource(source, ...)`).
2. **The question's parameter space is the space of term coefficients, and the report's `H(s, p) = T − S − F + sV⁻¹` is a two-dimensional slice of it.** The report's family has no coefficient on fission, so it cannot pose k (it poses k separately, as `(T − S)ψ = (1/k)Fψ`). The general statement: the balance is `Σ_j c_j T_j(p)`, where the `c_j` are AFFINE coefficients on the terms (fission's `1/k`, all emission's `c`, the time term's `s`, an absorber's density) anchored at the physical point, and `p` are NON-AFFINE parameters inside the terms (a size, a temperature, a control-rod position). An affine direction through the anchor gives a pencil (k, c, α, noise); a non-affine one gives a nonlinear eigenproblem. This keeps the report's `s` and `p` and adds the carriers of the review.
3. **A fixed source AT a pole is a question of its own, with a solvability law.** The report's quadrant 2 ("singular perturbation") is new to this plan: a source posed where the operator is singular, solvable only if the source is orthogonal to the adjoint null vector (the Fredholm alternative, `⟨ψ†, q⟩ = 0`), with the null-space component left free (a gauge). Its instances are real: generalized perturbation theory's importance equations and a static feedback source in a critical core are both posed at the k-normalised point, which is a pole by construction. `consumers_step2_design.md` §3.5 named the missing `admits(q)` Fredholm test; this is its question. The report's axis (at a pole or not) is orthogonal to the step-2 cells (an operator `M` or not, a source `q` or not): pole-ness is a property of the evaluation point relative to the spectrum. A question can declare it only when the point is a pole by construction (the normalised anchor); otherwise it is known only after solving, and the Strategy's certificate reports it.
4. **Moving between quadrants is a map between questions, with explicit data flow.** "Homogeneous to inhomogeneous" (solve the eigenproblem, then pose perturbation sources from ψ₀, k and ψ†) and "inhomogeneous to homogeneous" (strip the source, pose the eigenproblem of the same Problem) are the review's re-anchoring law made concrete: one question's answer supplies another question's anchor and source.
5. **The non-affine critical search is a root over pencils, and its local linearisation is a pencil in the INCREMENT.** Linearising `A(p)ψ = 0` at `p₀` gives `A(p₀)ψ = (p − p₀)Bψ` with `B = −∂A/∂p`: the eigenvalue is the Newton step `δp`, not `p` (the report writes `p·Bψ`). Consequence for the term set: its terms must be functions of the non-affine parameters, not frozen values, so that `∂A/∂p` exists (by automatic differentiation or by hand); a seed for #394 (JAX).
6. **Condensation has two roles, which the report merges.** A Petrov–Galerkin coarse operator used to accelerate one solve (DSA, coarse-mesh correction) keeps the question and is the Strategy's; a condensation that produces coarse operators for a new pass poses a NEW, coarser Problem (homogenisation), which answers a different question. The report's loop "feed the condensed operators back into the middle layer" is the second.
7. **The adjoint through automatic differentiation.** A vector–Jacobian product is the Euclidean transpose; the physical adjoint is `♯ ∘ dual ∘ ♭` (the Riesz maps of the metric). A custom VJP for the sweep must therefore implement the metric adjoint, which is what the report intends ("Riesz representation operators") and what `.H` already is.

**What it gets wrong or leaves unspellable** (refuted candidates, each with its reason):
- **The automatic "multiphysics supervisor" that restores α when the Fredholm condition fails:** it changes the question (from a static solve to a transient with an unknown growth rate) inside the machinery, which the question/Strategy gate forbids (a Strategy choice may not change the answer). The honest form is an explicit question: the source problem with α as an additional unknown, a bordered (augmented) system `[A(λ₀) + αT, …; ⟨ψ†, ·⟩, 0]`, posed by the user.
- **The quadrant names `InversionBase`, `IterationSource`, `SystemOperator`, `WeightOperator`:** Strategy vocabulary (inversion, iteration) in the question; names by role where the structure is a pole-search or a point evaluation.
- **The formula `A(p₀)ψ = p·Bψ`:** the eigenvalue is `δp` (item 5).
- **The reference implementation:** a toy, not a reference: its operators are mock arithmetic, `jresidual` is undefined, and the ψ update (subtract the normalised residual) is not a valid eigen-update. It illustrates the layering, not the numerics.
- **Quadrant 3 as "bypassed":** it is excluded by the refusal of a trivial question, which the question type can make unspellable (a homogeneous question off a pole has only ψ = 0).

**Where the report and this plan agree** (no change): three layers with a one-way dependency; no "loss"/"gain" names on the Problem (they are the k question's roles); complexification at the question (report v2 §I.4: the ground field is declared at posing, and the cone does not survive it); the partition, splitting and lowering on the Strategy; the α problem as a pole-search in `s`; noise as the resolvent at `s = iω`.

## Modes, equilibria and the spectrum (2026-09-26, for the user's ruling on the transition carrier) `[R]`

1. **α does not need k.** The α question is `Eigen(time)` from the anchor: `(A − F + α/v)ψ = 0`, with `α` the pole along the time direction. It can be solved directly (an eigen solve on the time pencil, shift-invert near the expected α) or, as a Strategy, by the k–α iteration (find α such that the k of the balance with the extra `α/v` term is 1), which uses k machinery as a root search and is NOT a precondition of the question. Its fundamental is the rightmost real α whose mode is in the positive cone (the spectral bound of the positive semigroup), which is why `Fundamental` is defined by the cone and not by a chart. Caveat the documentation must carry: in continuous transport a strongly subcritical or very small system may have NO discrete fundamental α (the α spectrum becomes continuous below `−min(vΣ_t)`, Lehner and Wing); a discretised system always has eigenvalues, so an α below that line is a discretisation artefact, and the question must refuse or flag it. Negative α also makes the `α/v` term an emission, which breaks the k–α iteration's positivity; the direct solve does not care.
2. **α answers a different and in one sense stronger question than k.** The k answer makes the balance singular by scaling fission, so its mode is the stationary state of a FICTITIOUS system (ν divided by k). The α answer makes it singular by moving along time, and a shift along time is an exponential modulation (the Laplace shift theorem), so its mode gives an exact solution of the REAL, unmodified time-dependent problem: `ψ(t) = e^{αt}ψ_α`, precursors included at their own α-equilibrium. A composition search (boron) gives the stationary state of a real, physically adjusted system. So every fundamental pole gives a base state, and they are three different base states: fictitious-stationary (k, c), real-separable (α), real-stationary (composition, geometry).
3. **So the carrier is a MODE, of which the equilibrium is the special case.** A pole of the family along a direction, with its Laurent datum (the residue `ψ ⊗ ψ†`), is a `Mode`. Every eigen answer is one or more modes. The fundamental mode additionally carries the physical scale (the power level) and is a base state for the questions built on it; the parent question (its direction) says which kind of base state, which is exactly why a derived question names its parent by value (noise around the k-normalised state and noise around the real exponentially-growing state are different questions with different answers). For the α base state, the derived questions are posed at the ANCHOR with the base state carried by modulation: the response to a perturbation of the real system at frequency ω around `e^{αt}ψ_α` is the fixed source at `s = α + iω`, with the source `−δΣ ψ_α`. The k base state is posed at the k point. One carrier, and the direction decides the point: this is the structure review's "the direction of departure is the new question's own datum" read from the other side.
4. **The spectrum.** Solving for more than the fundamental (`Enclosed(region)`, or several `Nearest`) returns several modes. The harmonics are not base states (they are not in the cone and carry no physical scale) but they are the other poles of the same resolvent, so they are exactly what a modal expansion uses: `R(s) ≈ Σ_n ψ_n ⊗ ψ_n† / (s − s_n)` plus a regular part. That gives the modal (λ-mode or α-mode) synthesis of noise and transients, the dominance ratio (a Strategy's convergence diagnostic and a question's physical one, the harmonic separation), and higher-harmonic perturbation theory. The k-harmonics and the α-harmonics are different bases (poles of different directions), so a mode names its direction too. The resolvent was ruled to be the ONE object both question kinds use; a spectrum is that object's pole set, so this adds no concept beyond the `Mode`.
5. **Proposed carrier, replacing amendment 1's `Equilibrium`:** an eigen Solution's outcome holds its `Mode`s (pole, direction, ψ, ψ†, and, for the fundamental, the scale); `solution.fundamental()` is the base state; derived questions (`Noise`, `Transient`, the at-pole source, GPT) name their parent question by value and read the base state from it; the harmonic modes are available to a modal Strategy or a modal analysis. `Equilibrium` survives as the name of the zero-frequency case if it is wanted as a word, or retires.
