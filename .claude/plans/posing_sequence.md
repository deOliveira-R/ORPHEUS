## The ontology as it stands (re-consolidated 2026-09-28, second pass, after the second clean-context attack and its rulings; the first 2026-09-28 text is at `451a5a5a` in git, the 2026-09-27 text at `c2bac2e6`, the 2026-09-26 text at `f6e35db2`; the reasoning and the evidence are in the sections below, from "The clean-context attack" on, the latest in "The second clean-context attack")

This section is meant to be read alone. Every statement is RULED unless marked `[OPEN]` (undecided) or `[PROPOSED]` (a clean-attack finding folded in for the next attack, not yet ruled one by one), and a ruling's date is given where it was made after 2026-09-27. Notation, used throughout:
- `B` is the boundary term, `T_time = 1/v` the time term, and `T_d` the derivative of an equation family along a direction `d`.
- `R(·)` is the resolvent only; `ρ` is the reactivity only (the frame section below writes the density of one measure against another in words, never as `ρ`).
- `s` is the Laplace variable of time; `α` a time eigenvalue (a pole in `s`); `ω` a real frequency, `s = iω` (noise); `Λ` the prompt neutron generation time.
- `c` names a critical-coefficient search: the scale on one cell's coefficient (an absorber concentration, say) at which the system is critical. "The k line" and "along c" are the lines through the base point along the fission-emission direction and along that cell's direction.
- The CONTINUUM EDGE of a direction is where the continuous problem's spectrum stops being discrete (for α, `Re α < −min(vΣ_t)`, Lehner–Wing); a discretisation replaces what lies beyond it by a band of pseudospectral poles.
- A TERM is one role's slice of the reaction grid (all reactions); a CHANNEL is one reaction's slice (all roles).
- Rulings are cited by date; labels such as "ruling (a)" belong to the discussion sections below.
- A *measure* is weights on points (it says how to integrate). A *basis* is a family of functions. A *frame* binds a basis to a measure and yields an analysis face (measure nodes → coefficients) and a reconstruction face (coefficients → values at the measure's nodes).

### The input layer: data and the reaction grid

- The input layer holds the (reaction × role) grid of cross-section data (`Mixture`). A reaction's part of the balance is a removal multiplier minus an emission kernel: `M[Σ_x] − ν_x K_x`, with `K_x = Σ_x P_x` and `P_x` a Markov kernel (loss minus gain per process). A CELL is one (reaction, role) entry. Today there are 8 non-zero cells: 5 removal, 3 emission (scattering, (n,2n), fission). The fission emission's multiplicity is a vector over target systems summing to ν̄: prompt to the neutron system, delayed to the precursor system.
- **The grid stores reactions and multiplicities only** (2026-09-28). Every sum over reactions is a derived view, so the stored `SigT` and `SigP` retire. The imbalanced producers the tree has today are re-spelled class by class:
  - placeholder scaffolds declare one named removal reaction (radiative capture);
  - the billiard carrier gets a real fission cell `(Σ_f, ν)`;
  - the Atalay encoding (a scattering ratio `c_s > 1`) becomes `Σ_f > 0` with `ν = 1 + (c_s − 1)Σ_t/Σ_f`, and `make_mixture` stops taking `sig_t`. `[M]` This preserves `c_s` only with `Σ_f := Σ_a` and `Σ_c := 0` (otherwise 1.1 becomes 0.81). `[PROPOSED]` A pure scatterer with `c_s > 1` is a scattering cell with multiplicity above 1, which the grid's stored multiplicities admit;
  - the guard's own tests become the gates that the imbalanced state cannot be written.
- **Cells are named by the reaction they are** (2026-09-28). `SigC` is radiative capture (MT102), not "capture" in the literature's derived sense (absorption minus fission). `SigL` is (n,α) (MT107) only. A derived sum takes two words.
- **Two totals; the derived one lazy, checked at one gate** (2026-09-28, re-ruled after the second attack).
  - `derived_SigT` is the removal sum over the reaction set a consumer actually uses. That consumer derives it lazily: the collision operator for a deterministic method, and, for Monte Carlo, the flight structure, which is the same collision operator read through its sampleable representation. The library cannot derive it, because it does not know which reactions will be modelled.
  - `library_SigT` is the library's physical total (MT1), including reactions no operator models. It stays at the nuclide level, where the σ0 self-shielding iteration consumes it.
  - The comparison is ONE method on the library. It takes a derived total as its argument, compares it with `library_SigT`, and emits a WARNING that non-trivial reactions have been omitted, with the size of the discrepancy. Every consumer calls it. `[PROPOSED]` The method states the level it compares at: the library total is collapsed over the same composition as the derived total.
  - The EAGER form is the named successor, adopted when Monte Carlo is built: a declared reaction-set object, built from the materials before any operator, derives the total once and runs the check once. The move is a reorganisation at the head of layer 1, not a change of layer 1.
  - `[M]` Today the micro total is summed from its components (`gendf.py:476`), and MT1 is not read. `library_SigT` requires reading MT1 from the tape; otherwise the check compares a sum with itself.
- `[OPEN]` The exact collapsed collision operator is diagonal in the ordinates, with a direction-dependent coefficient that a finer calculation must supply. It is not rotation-invariant and not diagonal in the harmonics (#518). Its adjoint weighting (ψ against ψ†) bears on deriving `.H` from one operator.

### Layer 1: systems, each carrying its equation

- **A system is an equation family** (2026-09-27). A system is:
  - its spaces, with their measures and metric, so `.H` and the Riesz legs are defined;
  - its equation family `E(p)` over its OWN parameter space, with `E(p)x = q` the equation at the point `p`. This is a holomorphic operator family (Kato; a nonlinear eigenvalue problem when non-affine);
  - the derivative `T_d(p)` along any declared direction;
  - its declared coordinates, each named in the system's own vocabulary (`fission_emission`, `time`, an extent, a composition field). Each declares:
    - its AFFINITY per chart and per reduction (a declaration, verified by a second-difference gate, never decided by a tolerance);
    - its CHART;
    - its ADMISSIBLE RANGE, whose boundary is the end `Fundamental` starts from (2026-09-28). For an emission coordinate it is 0, the chart's zero (fission off): `[M]` on 2 fixtures (one shipped, one scratch) `(A − σF)⁻¹ ≥ 0` exactly on `[0, 1/k)` and never for `σ < 0`;
    - its CONTINUUM EDGE, read by `Enclosed` and by the seed clamp (2026-09-28);
    - the sign of `⟨1, T_d ·⟩` (whether the direction is definite; an indefinite one refuses `Fundamental`);
  - which coordinate is time, with its mass operator `T_time`;
  - its cone, a positivity predicate on its unknowns, and its BULK sub-block, the block on which the cone and `Fundamental` are read (2026-09-28);
  - its ports, the terms through which it couples to another system;
  - `physical_point`, the point at which every coordinate takes its physical value (2026-09-28; it was `anchor`, which is a metaphor that already names the verification pin).
- The neutron system realises `E` for a method as `balance(p) = L − B + s·T_time + Σ_cells a_cell(p) · T_cell`, with `T_cell` signed (a removal cell `+M[Σ_x]`, an emission cell `−ν_x K_x`). At `physical_point` that is `L + C − S − N_2n − F − B`. While no precursor system is declared, `F` is the whole fission emission (total ν̄). Once it is declared, the neutron system's own `F` is the PROMPT part, the delayed part is the arrow into the precursor system, and total ν̄ returns only after the reduction at `s = 0` (the system algebra, below).
  - This term list is the FIRST-ORDER-FORM realisation, not the layer's contract. Diffusion's `D = 1/(3Σ_tr)` and collision probability's escape probabilities are not affine in the cells.
  - The terms are the grid's role-marginals (`C = Σ_x M[Σ_x]`; `S`, `N_2n`, `F` one emission cell each), a channel is its reaction-marginal, and the cell → term map is each term's own constructor, derived (gate: `array_equal` against today's terms).
- **Scalar cell coefficients are constant sections of FIELD coefficients** (the multiplier algebra of cell fields). This is what lets one region's absorber worth be posed. Unshielded compositions are affine; self-shielded ones are not.
- **The precursor system** is seeded, not built as a multi-system solve. Its space, its decay, its time term and its two arrows are declared, with the trivial boundary law at rest. Its source is the fission cell's DELAYED arrow (so `βνΣ_f` belongs to the fission cell), and its sink `λC` is the delayed emission into the neutron system. Advection and diffusion of precursors are not coded: their boundary law depends on whether the whole loop is declared.
- **The discretisation** is the product of per-axis frames: space (mesh and spatial scheme), angle (quadrature and angular closure), energy (group structure). It carries CONTENT identity (a digest), so a loaded Solution's discretisation can be recognised. Names:
  - `SNDiscretization`, `DiffusionDiscretization` and `InfiniteMediumDiscretization` (the package `orpheus/homogeneous/` becomes `orpheus/infinite_medium/`);
  - `CPDiscretization` and `MOCDiscretization` come with their own campaigns;
  - the Protocol is `Discretization` (was `TransportMethod`, `transport/method.py:150`). `[M]` It carries 0 of `TransportMethod`'s 4 members; `[OPEN]` the home of the boundary-law realisation and of `resolve_boundary_conditions`, `TransportMethod`'s one consumer;
  - the materials are a field of the discretisation (has-a), not its base class. `[OPEN]` The material field is state, so its home may be the system's `physical_point` (the field coordinates) instead; `material_at(point)` on the geometry is a third spelling of the same lookup. One home is owed.
- **Both frame faces are declared on `Discretization`** (2026-09-28).
  - The ANALYSIS face goes from a method-free field (a source, say) to the coefficients; its verb is `analysis` (below), whatever it is applied to.
  - The RECONSTRUCTION face goes from the coefficients to a function on the continuous phase space. It is realised by the frame's basis where the representation is modal (harmonic moments, a linear-discontinuous polynomial, finite-volume cell averages), and by the characteristic solve `(L_Ω + C)⁻¹(Sφ + q)` where it is nodal (discrete ordinates; diamond difference's face value).
  - The energy reconstruction's within-group shape is the condensation's within-group spectrum (`WithinGroupSpectrum`).
  - `[M]` Today the reconstruction face exists for 1 of 10 axis factors (harmonic moments), is partial for 3 and absent for 6. There is no product of frames. So this is machinery to build.
- **The trace** (2026-09-28, re-ruled after the second attack). The boundary trace is its own space with its own frame: face nodes times a half-range angular measure, under the Stokes weight `|Ω·n|` that `AngularTraceSpace` already carries (`angular_trace_space.py:281-310`); diffusion's `J±` are its half-range zeroth moments under that weight. Bulk and trace are the two summands of a COPRODUCT (disjoint-union) manifold, which is built. The code already realises it (`[M]` 2026-09-28 on a 3-cell S2 slab, a sphere and a diffusion slab): the loss on bulk ⊕ trace is one 2×2 block operator, the trace holds inflow and outflow as independent unknowns, and there is no bulk → trace gather.
  - Trace → bulk (`A_bt`, a block of the streaming or leakage leaf `L`) is the boundary datum of the characteristic solve, the same object as the nodal reconstruction face.
  - Bulk → trace (`A_tb`, the trace rows of `L`) is `M_∂ ∘ γ ∘ R_V`: the scheme's reconstruction face, read on the boundary by the continuous trace `γ` and analysed in the trace frame (diamond difference: `2ψ̄ − ψ_in`, chained cell to cell).

  - The commuting condition `A_tb ∘ M_V = M_∂ ∘ γ` holds on the closure's reproducing space only, and is gated there (`[M]` diamond difference is exact on `x` and off by −h²/3 on `x²`).
  - No pullback of `∂V ↪ V` is typed: a cell-centred scheme has no face → cell node map. `TraceRestrictionOperator` is the outflow selector of the face trace, not a bulk → trace map.
- **The system algebra** (2026-09-27) sits between layer 1 and layer 2. It is generic machinery named by structure, and its outputs are systems:
  - COUPLE several systems through their ports, as block operators;
  - REDUCE a subsystem at a point by Schur elimination. This covers the trace unknowns, which carry no time term (`[M]` `T_time` has rank 40 of 48 on a diffusion slab), so the time family on bulk ⊕ trace is a differential-algebraic pencil until the trace is eliminated. It covers the precursors at a point that the binder supplies, either exactly with fuel at rest (the delayed emission `λ_i/(s + λ_i)·β_i νΣ_f φ`; at `s = 0` the total-ν balance with `χ_eff = (1 − β)χ_p + Σ β_i χ_{d,i}`, and `C` recovered by back-substitution) or under a stated time approximation (quasi-static, prompt jump). The reduced balance is rational in `s`, so on the neutron system alone the time family is not a pencil once delayed neutrons are present. The reduced adjoint equals the adjoint of the reduction (a gate);
  - RESTRICT to a subsystem;
  - PULL BACK or FIX a coordinate (a composition field pulled back to cell coefficients; a coordinate frozen);
  - COMPLEXIFY.
  Every output DERIVES its declarations (mass operator, affinity per coordinate, admissible range, cone, bulk block, ports) from its inputs' (2026-09-28). A reduction at `s` has a rational affinity in `s` and no constant mass operator, and says so; a complexification has no cone.
- **The Monte Carlo seam** (2026-09-27; declarations only). Monte Carlo is a layer-3 Strategy over the same system; no stochastic decision is made at layer 1. The seam:
  - identity factors on the axes a method leaves undiscretised;
  - a `markov_view(point)` member of the system (the free flight over `derived_SigT` and the geometry, the collision kernel over the reaction set, the boundary kernel, the source measure), with no `sample` verb on a term;
  - the free flight is the Method of Characteristics' Volterra operator (a direction ruled 2026-09-28). On one characteristic, `(L + C)⁻¹` has kernel `e^{−τ}`, and its collision-density form `C(L + C)⁻¹` has kernel `Σ_t e^{−τ}`, a sub-probability density on the ray (the missing mass is the escape probability).   - the emission kernel `P_x` typed as a Markov kernel with two representations (a Legendre image that can be applied but may be signed, and a sampleable one);
  - `outcome.read(functional)`, and an `Estimated(value, standard_error, n_eff)` kind of evidence. `[M]` 2 of 2 production readers of the evidence kinds drop an unknown kind silently (`isinstance(x, Measured)` with a silent else); those dispatches become exhaustive before `Estimated` lands;

  - `reconstruction`: the synthesis `c ↦ Σ c_k φ_k` at the measure's nodes.
  - `dual_analysis`: `(MR)⁻¹M`, the analysis of the dual that the test/trial PAIR determines; the canonical dual `G⁻¹M` iff test = trial. The docstring names the sense, because "dual" is spent on the algebraic dual.

### Layer 2: the problem (a question bound to a system)

- **Questions are physics-free** (2026-09-27). A question names a coordinate of the system it is bound to as an opaque key; k is the system's name for its fission-emission direction. Because nothing in a question names a reaction cell, the problem types can live in `numerics`. The physical names stay in the systems.
- **Three kinds, `<Kind>Problem` over a value `<Kind>(…)`, each with one `point` slot** (the single slot is `[PROPOSED]`):
  - `EigenProblem` over `Eigen(parameter, point, mode)`: where, on the line through `point` along the parameter's direction, is `E` singular?
  - `FixedSourceProblem` over `FixedSource(source, point)`: `E(point)⁻¹q`, for a source that does not depend on the unknown.
  - `EvolutionProblem` over `Evolution(initial, source, f, point)`: the initial-value question, whose answer is a function of the generator applied to the initial state. A transient from a critical state is posed at the k pole, so it needs its point.
- **The parameter** is a declared coordinate of the system: a `CoefficientDirection`, `GeometryExtent`, or `NuclideDensity(nuclide, regions)`. The value `Eigen` holds the parameter; the pencil and the spectral map are derived from it (`CriticalParameter` does not exist in the tree, and nothing is dissolved).
- **The point** carries two separate objects: the ALGEBRAIC ORIGIN of a line (where σ = 0) and the BASE POINT the question is posed at. `[M]` Re-posing from the k pole along c with the parameter's component "projected out" finds the c pole, not offset 0. The gate is answer-invariance under the point's own parameter component. `[PROPOSED]` The algebraic origin moves onto the coordinate's declaration (its chart zero, the boundary of its admissible range), so the single `point` slot carries only the base point; `pseudo_resolvent(0) = A⁻¹F` holds from the chart's zero, not from the base point.
- **The mode selector:** `Fundamental`, `Nearest(τ)` (τ read in the parameter's chart), `Enclosed(region)`; never `Index(n)`.
  - `Fundamental` is the first pole met from the removal-dominated end of the direction, on the BULK cone. That end is the boundary of the coordinate's declared admissible range (2026-09-28). `[M]` On the composite bulk ⊕ trace the positivity region is empty; on the bulk block it works, with the k line ending at 1/k and the α line at α₀.
  - A direction whose sign is indefinite (fuel plus moderator) refuses `Fundamental`.
  - Positivity is reported, not guaranteed: guaranteed for positive schemes, not for diamond difference.
  - `Enclosed` over a region beyond the direction's continuum edge (where poles are pseudospectral) is refused or flagged `[OPEN]` which; both attackers say refuse, now that the edge is declared.
- **The adjoint ψ†** of an eigen answer is the null vector of `balance(pole)†`. `[M]` The Hellmann–Feynman pole derivative agrees with finite differences to 4e-7 (α) and 5e-9 (k) with it, and is 21 % off with the iteration operator's left Perron vector. Hellmann–Feynman against finite differences is a gate.
- `[PROPOSED]` **At a pole**, a fixed-source question is its own type: `SingularSource(source, parent, departure)`, carrying the parent eigen question by value and the direction of departure `d` (time for static, noise and transient questions; fission emission for generalised perturbation theory). The gauge `⟨ψ†, T_d x⟩ = 0` is the question's projection. A source is admissible iff `⟨ψ†, q⟩ = 0` (the Fredholm condition); an inadmissible one grows secularly. Likewise, the fundamental's scale (the power level) is carried by a `FundamentalMode` type or by the outcome's gauge, never by an optional field on every mode. (The elegance attacker: `FundamentalMode` twins the existing `ScaleGauge`, so the outcome's gauge is the home; the structural attacker agrees.)
- **Time.** `generator` is the generator of the time family, `G = −T_time⁻¹ · E|_{s=0}`, on the space where that family is affine: the neutron system after the trace is eliminated for a prompt-only system; bulk ⊕ precursors with delayed neutrons. An evolution's `f` is either:
  - `Stepped(step, grid)`, the question's own time discretisation, a word in the terms' resolvents that owns its implicit/explicit labelling (so IMEX and splitting are admitted; the reduction's point depends on the scheme: `s = 1/Δt` for backward Euler, `2/Δt` for Crank–Nicolson). `[OPEN]` The labelling names terms, which a physics-free layer cannot; its home is `Stepped` (as ruled) or the Strategy's plan (layer 3);
  - or `Exact(output_times, tolerance)`, the continuous-time answer.
  A non-autonomous evolution admits only `Stepped` (a declared scope boundary). `[PROPOSED]` `Exact` with delayed neutrons waits, for a deterministic Strategy, for the multi-system machinery; the limit is the Strategy's, not the question's (a Monte Carlo Strategy samples precursors natively).
- **The pencil and the resolvent.** For an affine direction, the pencil is `E` restricted to the line: `at(σ) = unshifted + σ · T_d`, with `pencil.shifted(σ)` obeying `shifted(σ).at(τ) == at(σ + τ)`. A non-affine direction has `OperatorPencil.jet(family, p0, h)`.
  - `pseudo_resolvent(σ) = at(σ)⁻¹ · (−T_d)`. `[PROPOSED]` The next-generation operator is prose: the name `pseudo_resolvent(0)` takes when `d` is fission emission, not an accessor.

## Modes, equilibria and the spectrum (2026-09-26, for the user's ruling on the transition carrier) `[R]`

1. **α does not need k.** The α question is `Eigen(time)` from the anchor: `(A − F + α/v)ψ = 0`, with `α` the pole along the time direction. It can be solved directly (an eigen solve on the time pencil, shift-invert near the expected α) or, as a Strategy, by the k–α iteration (find α such that the k of the balance with the extra `α/v` term is 1), which uses k machinery as a root search and is NOT a precondition of the question. Its fundamental is the rightmost real α whose mode is in the positive cone (the spectral bound of the positive semigroup), which is why `Fundamental` is defined by the cone and not by a chart. Caveat the documentation must carry: in continuous transport a strongly subcritical or very small system may have NO discrete fundamental α (the α spectrum becomes continuous below `−min(vΣ_t)`, Lehner and Wing); a discretised system always has eigenvalues, so an α below that line is a discretisation artefact, and the question must refuse or flag it. Negative α also makes the `α/v` term an emission, which breaks the k–α iteration's positivity; the direct solve does not care.
2. **α answers a different and in one sense stronger question than k.** The k answer makes the balance singular by scaling fission, so its mode is the stationary state of a FICTITIOUS system (ν divided by k). The α answer makes it singular by moving along time, and a shift along time is an exponential modulation (the Laplace shift theorem), so its mode gives an exact solution of the REAL, unmodified time-dependent problem: `ψ(t) = e^{αt}ψ_α`, precursors included at their own α-equilibrium. A composition search (boron) gives the stationary state of a real, physically adjusted system. So every fundamental pole gives a base state, and they are three different base states: fictitious-stationary (k, c), real-separable (α), real-stationary (composition, geometry).
3. **So the carrier is a MODE, of which the equilibrium is the special case.** A pole of the family along a direction, with its Laurent datum (the residue `ψ ⊗ ψ†`), is a `Mode`. Every eigen answer is one or more modes. The fundamental mode additionally carries the physical scale (the power level) and is a base state for the questions built on it; the parent question (its direction) says which kind of base state, which is exactly why a derived question names its parent by value (noise around the k-normalised state and noise around the real exponentially-growing state are different questions with different answers). For the α base state, the derived questions are posed at the ANCHOR with the base state carried by modulation: the response to a perturbation of the real system at frequency ω around `e^{αt}ψ_α` is the fixed source at `s = α + iω`, with the source `−δΣ ψ_α`. The k base state is posed at the k point. One carrier, and the direction decides the point: this is the structure review's "the direction of departure is the new question's own datum" read from the other side.
4. **The spectrum.** Solving for more than the fundamental (`Enclosed(region)`, or several `Nearest`) returns several modes. The harmonics are not base states (they are not in the cone and carry no physical scale) but they are the other poles of the same resolvent, so they are exactly what a modal expansion uses: `R(s) ≈ Σ_n ψ_n ⊗ ψ_n† / (s − s_n)` plus a regular part. That gives the modal (λ-mode or α-mode) synthesis of noise and transients, the dominance ratio (a Strategy's convergence diagnostic and a question's physical one, the harmonic separation), and higher-harmonic perturbation theory. The k-harmonics and the α-harmonics are different bases (poles of different directions), so a mode names its direction too. The resolvent was ruled to be the ONE object both question kinds use; a spectrum is that object's pole set, so this adds no concept beyond the `Mode`.
5. **Proposed carrier, replacing amendment 1's `Equilibrium`:** an eigen Solution's outcome holds its `Mode`s (pole, direction, ψ, ψ†, and, for the fundamental, the scale); `solution.fundamental()` is the base state; derived questions (`Noise`, `Transient`, the at-pole source, GPT) name their parent question by value and read the base state from it; the harmonic modes are available to a modal Strategy or a modal analysis. `Equilibrium` survives as the name of the zero-frequency case if it is wanted as a word, or retires.

## When the fundamental α does not exist (2026-09-26) `[R]` throughout; references owed by a literature pass

1. **The line.** For the time family of the continuous transport balance, the region `Re α ≤ λ* = −min(vΣ_t)` is continuous spectrum (the uncollided streaming part alone fills it: a neutron that never collides decays as `e^{−vΣ_t t}`, for every direction). Above the line the spectrum is discrete. So a fundamental exists iff the family has a real pole above the line.
2. **The prediction is a k solve at the line.** Write `k(α)` for the k eigenvalue of the balance with the extra `α/v` term. For `α > λ*` it is decreasing in α, and the fundamental α is the root of `k(α) = 1`. Hence: the fundamental α exists iff `k(λ*⁺) > 1`, i.e. the system with the time-absorption set to the line (its smallest removal made exactly zero) is still supercritical. A small, or leaky, or strongly subcritical system fails it. This is one k solve, the existence test a question can run before (or instead of) an α solve, and it reuses the ruled machinery: it is `Eigen(fission, at=anchor + λ*·time)`, a multi-parameter question.
3. **Where the line itself lives matters.** In multigroup, `λ* = −min_g v_g Σ_{t,g}`; with continuous energy and a cross section `Σ_t(E)` with `vΣ_t → 0` as `E → 0` (constant scattering at low energy), `λ* = 0`, so NO subcritical continuous-energy system is guaranteed a discrete α; the multigroup line is itself a discretisation's line (the Corngold limit of pulsed-neutron experiments in small moderator blocks is this edge). So the line is computed from the discretisation, and a statement about the continuous problem needs the energy treatment named.
4. **The discrete system always has a fundamental, so an artefact is predictable.** After discretisation, `−v(A − F)` is a finite matrix with non-negative off-diagonal part, so Perron–Frobenius gives a real rightmost eigenvalue with a non-negative mode ALWAYS: the discrete question never fails to answer. Where the continuous problem has no pole, the discrete answer is the discretisation's representation of the continuous spectrum's edge. Predictors: (a) the computed α lies at or below the discretisation's line `−min_g v_g Σ_{t,g}` (a one-line check); (b) the existence test of point 2 fails; (c) under angular refinement a genuine pole converges to a limit above the line while the artefact migrates toward the line (the finite quadrature's streaming modes `−v_g Σ_{t,g}` along each ordinate are what fill in the continuum); (d) the artefact's mode is streaming-dominated (mostly uncollided flux, concentrated on grazing or single ordinates), and its adjoint-mode overlap is small (the eigenproblem is strongly non-normal there, so pseudospectra are wide). Each is measurable; (a) and (b) are cheap enough to be the question's own refusal or flag.
5. **What the artefact is good for.** Individually it is not a physical mode, but it is not noise either: (a) its limit under refinement is the continuum edge `λ*`, which is the physically observed asymptotic decay rate of a pulsed small system (the decay is then not purely exponential: an algebraic prefactor times `e^{λ* t}`); (b) collectively, the cluster of discrete poles near the line is a rational approximation of the resolvent's branch cut, so a modal sum or a contour integral over them still converges to the true time response: they are useful as a BASIS, not as a mode; (c) the existence boundary itself (the size or composition at which `k(λ*⁺) = 1`) is a physical quantity, the critical size for an α mode, computable with the same machinery.
6. **Consequence for the design:** the `Fundamental` selector on the time direction has three outcomes, not two: a pole above the line (a `Mode`), the edge (no mode; the answer is the continuum edge and the question says so), and a discrete pole below the line (an artefact, flagged by its own predictors). The `Mode` type needs a way to say "this pole is the edge of the essential spectrum", or the α question returns a result type that is a sum of those cases.

### Corrections from the literature pass (2026-09-27; `scratch/posing_sequence/alpha_existence/literature_memo.md`, page citations there; 14 PDFs added to `scratch/literature/`)

The section above is right in method and wrong in four load-bearing facts:
- Point 1 `[REFUTED 2026-09-27]` except for two cases. A continuous spectrum below `−min(vΣ_t)` exists only when paths can be arbitrarily long (the slab, infinite laterally: grazing directions) or when speeds reach zero (continuous energy). In a BOUNDED body with speeds bounded away from zero (one-speed or multigroup) there is no continuous spectrum; genuine real α's lie below the line (Van Norton 1962; Corngold 1964 p. 86; Betzler et al. 2018 p. 119; Carlvik 1968's sphere fundamental reaches 14× vΣ, reproduced to 5–6 digits `[M]` by the researcher), and Bell & Glasstone p. 374: "In the multigroup treatment, α₀ exists even for arbitrarily small systems." Genuine non-existence is a thermalisation effect of continuous energy (Mockel's minimum-thickness theorem, Corngold's maximum-B² theorem p. 84, Vidav 1968). Vidav's Theorem III (pp. 151–152) is the cone characterisation of the fundamental that `Fundamental` assumes.
- Point 2 stands in substance and is published (Dahl, Protopopescu & Sjöstrand 1983 p. 376; Mockel's Eqs. (5)–(7); the dispersion relation of Dorning's chapter, Eqs. ## Does solving k first help α? (2026-09-26; the user, verbatim: *"While the alpha eigenvalue problem does not need the k eigenvalue to be solved first, is there an advantage in having the k-eigenvalue solved first? For example, is the k fundamental mode useful to seed the alpha eigenvalue problem?"*) `[R]`, being measured (`scratch/posing_sequence/alpha_existence/`)

1. **Everything a k answer gives the α solve is Strategy data.** A seed, a shift and a bracket change how fast and to which pole the solve goes, never the answer, so the k answer SEEDS the α Strategy; it does not base the α question (the α question is posed at the anchor either way). This is a third way one answer serves another question, beside "bases it" (a derived question's base state) and "sources it" (a perturbation source): an answer seeds a Strategy. The Strategy transfer criterion ruled with amendment 8 allows it (an initial iterate and a shift, off a pole).
2. **What k gives:** (a) the sign of α for free: `α > 0` iff `k* > 1`, since `k(α)` decreases and `k(0) = k*`, so `[0, ∞)` or `(λ*, 0]` brackets the root; (b) a first-order estimate: along the time direction at α = 0, the Hellmann–Feynman derivative of the k family gives `α₀ ≈ (k* − 1)/(k* Λ)` with the adjoint-weighted generation time `Λ = ⟨ψ†, ψ/v⟩/⟨ψ†, Fψ⟩` (the reactivity over the generation time; the exact constant is being derived and measured), which is the natural `Nearest(τ)` shift for a shift-invert α solve; (c) the k mode as the initial iterate: the α mode equals the k mode at `k* = 1`, and they separate as `|k* − 1|` grows, because the `α/v` term weights the slow groups (the α mode is softer than the k mode for α > 0, harder for α < 0); (d) ψ† from the k solve, a good weight for the α problem's gauge and pairing near critical.
3. **Where it can hurt:** in the strongly subcritical regime a k-mode seed may steer a solve toward the continuum edge or an artefact, and the k–α iteration itself fails when α < 0 makes `α/v` an emission large enough to break positivity; the existence test (a k solve at the line) is then the useful k-side computation, not the seed.
4. **Design consequence:** a Strategy may take another Solution's `Mode` as a seed (`strategy.seeded_by(mode)`, spelling open), subject to content identity of the discretisation; the α question's result does not depend on it, and a gate can check that (the same α from a flat seed and from the k seed, to the solve tolerance).

## The complex plane (2026-09-27; the user, verbatim: *"Is it correct to understand the time as living in the complex plane, and a problem such as time evolution as a problem departing through its real axis? are there uses for the complex axis beyond traditional neutron noise analysis? Is this a broader question than my current understanding? For example, are there other parameters where Complexification and analysis on the complex plane is useful?"*) `[R]` throughout, references owed

1. **Correction of the picture.** Time is real; what lives in the complex plane is its Laplace dual `s`, the coefficient of the time term. The answer to a time question is an analytic function of `s`, the resolvent `R(s) = at(s)⁻¹`, and the regions of the plane are the questions: the real poles are the α modes (their real part is growth or decay); the imaginary axis `s = iω` is steady oscillation (noise, a frequency response); a real point `s = 1/Δt` is one implicit-Euler step (backward Euler samples the resolvent at a real point: `(1/Δt − G)⁻¹`, a rational approximation of `e^{Δt G}`); and the exact time evolution is the inverse Laplace transform, a contour integral `ψ(t) = (1/2πi) ∮ e^{st} R(s) q̂(s) ds` along a vertical line to the right of every pole (Bromwich), which may be deformed into a Talbot or hyperbolic contour. So time evolution does not "depart along the real axis": time stepping samples the resolvent at real points, and the exact evolution integrates it along a vertical contour.
2. **Uses beyond noise, for time's dual:** (a) contour-integral time integration: the transient at chosen output times as a weighted sum of fixed-source solves at complex `s` on a Talbot contour, each independent (parallel in time), with no time-step error (the error is the contour quadrature's, exponentially convergent); (b) the Chebyshev rational approximation method (CRAM, Pusa) for depletion: `e^{At}` of the Bateman matrix as a sum of resolvents at complex poles, the standard burnup solver, directly relevant to `fuel/` and `data/`; (c) stability of a reactor with feedback: the transfer function's poles in the right half-plane (the Nyquist criterion, xenon spatial oscillations, the boiling-water-reactor decay ratio); (d) the pulsed-neutron, Rossi-α and Feynman-α experiments, which measure the real poles.
3. **Other parameters, the broader principle:** any parameter that enters the balance analytically (polynomially or rationally) can be complexified, and the answer is then an analytic function of it whose singularities (poles and branch points) govern the real behaviour:
   - the eigen parameter itself: contour-integral eigensolvers (Sakurai–Sugiura, FEAST, Beyn) find every pole inside a closed contour, which is the ruled `Enclosed(region)`; the non-affine family's Keldysh structure makes it uniform (structure memo);
   - a small imaginary step on ANY real parameter (a cross section, a density, a thickness) gives the exact derivative, `f'(p) = Im f(p + ih)/h` to machine precision with no subtractive cancellation: complex-step differentiation, a sensitivity and uncertainty tool with no adjoint needed, and an independent check (X4) of the adjoint-weighted sensitivities;
   - the energy variable: resonance cross sections as sums of poles in the complex √E plane (R-matrix, the windowed multipole representation), with Doppler broadening analytic through the Faddeeva function; this is the data layer's native form of a resonance;
   - the spatial variable: a Fourier or Bloch phase `e^{iB·x}` (a complex buckling) poses leakage in an infinite lattice (the B1 method, a Bloch-periodic boundary law with a phase) and is exactly the tool of the Fourier analysis of iterative schemes (source iteration, DSA: the spectral radius read from complex modes), which is where D1–D3 are verified;
   - the angular variable: Case's singular-eigenfunction method: the dispersion function is analytic in the plane cut along `[−1, 1]`; for a multiplying medium (`c > 1`) its discrete root is imaginary, giving the oscillatory spatial mode whose fit to the slab is the criticality condition;
   - continuation in any parameter: eigenvalue branches meet at exceptional points (Kato) in the COMPLEX parameter plane; the distance to the nearest one is the radius of convergence of perturbation theory in that parameter and explains avoided crossings of harmonics, which is what a continuation Strategy must steer around.
4. **The unification for the ontology:** the question space was ruled the space of term coefficients (plus extents and compositions); the broader statement is that it is a COMPLEX space, with the physical questions on its real part and time's dual one coordinate. The ruled "complexification belongs to the question, the point's field decides" already admits it. One design consequence to decide: an initial-value question must say whether it asks for the answer of its own time discretisation (time stepping; the step is answer-changing, so the question's) or the continuous-time answer at given output times (then a Talbot contour is one Strategy and time stepping with an error target another).

## What a transient question asks: its own time discretisation, or the continuous-time answer? (2026-09-27; the user asked for the pros and cons, and whether the two are a choice or alternative machineries) `[R]`

**A. The answer of its own time discretisation** (ruled earlier: the grid and scheme are the question's). The answer after n implicit-Euler steps is `ψ_n = (I − Δt G)⁻ⁿ ψ₀`, with `G` the generator. Pros: consistent with how ORPHEUS treats space and angle (the mesh and quadrature are part of the posing; the continuous answer is approached by refinement ladders in verification, never asked for); the answer is exact up to the linear solves, so its certificate is the solves' certificates; it is the only form that survives time-varying coefficients and nonlinear feedback (a rod moving, thermal feedback), where each step re-evaluates the balance; the time order of accuracy is a question-level claim a gate can pin. Cons: two transients with different Δt are different questions; the answer carries a discretisation error the user must control by refinement; a method that computes the continuous answer (a Talbot contour, CRAM) answers a DIFFERENT question and would be refused.

**B. The continuous-time answer at given output times, to a stated tolerance:** the answer is `e^{tG} ψ₀` (plus the Duhamel integral of a source). Pros: the question is the physics; the methods become interchangeable Strategies (time stepping with an error estimate, a Talbot contour, CRAM, rational Krylov, a modal sum where the modes are well conditioned); parallel-in-time evaluation; methods can be compared on one target. Cons: the answer is known only to the tolerance, which needs an a posteriori error estimate (and the convergence contract's standing lesson is that no budget certifies a tolerance, #340, so the estimate must be a measurement, not a step count); the Laplace structure exists only for an AUTONOMOUS linear system (coefficients constant in time), so contours and CRAM do not apply to feedback or a moving rod, and there only error-controlled stepping remains.

**They are one machinery with two questions, not a choice.** Both answers are a FUNCTION OF THE GENERATOR applied to the initial state, `f(G)ψ₀` (the functional calculus): B has `f = e^{t·}`, given exactly; A has `f = r`, the rational function its scheme defines on its grid (`r(z) = (1 − Δt z)⁻ⁿ` for implicit Euler; any implicit Runge–Kutta or fixed-grid BDF is rational too). Every rational function is evaluated by resolvents: partial fractions `r(G) = Σ c_j (z_j − G)⁻¹` are fixed-source solves at the poles `z_j`, which is exactly what CRAM does for the exponential, and a contour integral is the same with quadrature weights. So one machinery (resolvent evaluations with weights, which the ruled `at(σ)` and the fixed-source question already provide) serves both; the question states `f`, and the Strategy chooses how to evaluate it: sequential steps (when `f` factors into steps), partial fractions or a contour (parallel), or a Krylov method. For A with distinct-pole schemes, the parallel evaluation reproduces the sequential answer to the solve tolerance, which is a gate. For B, `f = exp` is approximated, so the Strategy's approximation error enters the certificate.
Proposal: the transient question carries `f` as a value: `Evolution(initial, source, Exact(output_times, tolerance))` or `Evolution(initial, source, Scheme(step, grid))`; a non-autonomous evolution (coefficients varying in time, feedback) admits only `Scheme` today, a declared scope boundary naming the missing machinery (error-controlled stepping of a non-autonomous system). This also makes the minted `generator` load-bearing: a transient is `f(generator)`.
