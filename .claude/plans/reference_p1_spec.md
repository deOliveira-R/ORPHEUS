## 0. What changed from the plan (read first)

Rulings received during this specification (the orchestrator, 2026-09-25, reported to the user):

1. **The carve order is swapped at steps 6 and 7, and step 6 is renamed.** Step 6 is now *the phase-space functions* (`Symbolic`, `RegionwiseConstant`, the anisotropy predicate, `srepr` storage), named by structure, not by role. Step 7 is the question, whose adjoint `FixedSource` holds a detector of that function type. The specification's fields name the roles (`source`, `detector`). Reason: the adjoint fixed-source question needs a detector, which is a mesh-free function on phase space, the same kind of object as the source (§1.6, §1.7).
2. **The equal-width edge rule is R2, one body.** A spacing rule is affine in the measure coordinate `T(r)`: equal width uses `T(r) = r` in every coordinate system, equal volume uses `T(r) = r`, `r²`, `r³`. Edges are `T⁻¹(T(r_k) + f_j · (T(r_{k+1}) − T(r_k)))` with `f = linspace(0, 1, n+1)`, and both interval ends are the geometry's breakpoints exactly. On a slab the two rules are one code path, so #495 is fixed at its root. The rival R1 (`np.linspace(r_k, r_{k+1}, n+1)`) is refused because it moves the SN regression corpus (§3, §7).
3. **Hollow geometries are admitted.** The geometry is the interval of breakpoints `(r_0, …, r_R)` with `r_0 ≥ 0` for cylinder and sphere (any `r_0` for a slab). Its boundary is the topological boundary of that region in its coordinate system, so the number of boundary laws is derived: 2 for a slab; 1 for a cylinder or sphere with `r_0 = 0` (the centre is an interior point); 2 otherwise. A law at the centre is refused, and a hollow curvilinear geometry without an inner law is refused. `origin=` retires.
4. **`None` retires as a boundary declaration at step 3.** The geometry requires one law per boundary point. Each of today's `None` sites migrates to the law its consumer applied, and a second capture proves it (§3.2). The four consumer defaults (§0 item 8) are a weld and retire.
5. **#511 (filed by the orchestrator): SN silently ignores a declared inner law on a hollow curvilinear mesh.** At step 3 SN refuses any inner law other than `reflective`, with a `SCOPE-BOUNDARY[guard]` naming the missing machinery (an inner-surface trace on `RadialAxisMesh`); the curvilinear SN build is deferred by the user.
5b. **#513 (filed by the orchestrator): CP, MoC and MC read only some of a geometry's declared laws.** Ruling: every method that reads only some of the declared laws refuses a geometry whose other laws it would drop, a `SCOPE-BOUNDARY[guard]` recorded on #513 (CP and MC; the CP slab's mirror asymmetry is a comment there), #514 (MoC) and #511 (SN's inner surface); what a method realises on the unread face decides which law it admits there, and a site migrates to that law so it stays bit-identical (S3.12 to S3.14). #512 (a CP slab with a vacuum right law gives k = 1.9137, above k∞ = 1.875) is out of P1's scope.

Premise corrections measured while writing this specification:

6. **"Equal width: equal widths" is false bitwise for every realisable edge formula.** `[M]` `partition_laws.py`: the differences of `np.linspace` edges are not all equal in 772 of 852 (interval, n) cases, and the equal-volume sum of measures differs from the interval's closed-form measure by up to 6 ULP. The laws of §1.3 are therefore stated on the stored MEASURES (exact per rule) and on the realised widths within a derived ULP bound, never as bitwise equality of edge differences.
7. **"Every cell width ≤ h" is false for the natural count rule on realised edges.** `[M]` `maxwidth.py`: with `n = ceil(L/h)`, some realised width exceeds `h` in 14 of 114 399 (L, h) pairs, among them `L = 1, h = 0.1` (a width `0.10000000000000009`). ## 1. The ladder, per step

Each step lands as one commit, green under `.venv/bin/python -O -m pytest` on what it touches. For each gate: its claim kind (THEOREM: a law true for every admissible input; REFERENCE: a structurally independent route; RECORD: what the code printed on a given day), its first red (the input existing in the tree when it lands that the gate rejects, `plan-authoring` §6c; for a new type, its defining refusals), its mutation witness, its level, and the rungs it rests on. Every new, improved or reused gate carries `@pytest.mark.rests_on(...)` with the node ids named here. Unless stated otherwise a gate is `foundation` (a software or mathematical invariant with no theory-page label, so no `verifies`).

A refusal row is a `pytest.raises(<typed error>, match=<fragment>)` whose fragment is keyed to the argument that triggers it; the fragments of one constructor's refusals are disjoint, and one row asserts their disjointness (test-architect lessons §1, "the message IS the gate").

Names used below, defined once:

- **R, E, R†.** On a space whose factors are named axes, `R = space.retraction("angular")` is fibre integration over the angular axis, `(Rψ)(·) = Σₙ wₙ ψ(n, ·)`. `E = space.section("angular")` is its section, `(Eφ)(n, ·) = φ(·)/Σw`, so `R ∘ E = id`. `R†` is the Hilbert adjoint of `R`, the operator with `⟨Rψ, φ⟩ = ⟨ψ, R†φ⟩` in the two spaces' metrics.
- **The pullback π\*.** The plain broadcast `(π*φ)(n, ·) = φ(·)`, constant along the angular axis. In exact arithmetic `R† = π*` whenever the product condition below holds.
- **The sandwich.** The generic Hilbert adjoint every bound operator gets today, `♯_full ∘ Rᵀ ∘ ♭_marg` (`AdjointOperator(R)`): lower `φ` by the marginal metric, apply the Euclidean transpose `Rᵀφ = wₙ φ`, raise by the full metric.
- **The product condition.** The full space's metric is the product of the collapsed axis's own measure and the marginal space's metric, `G_full = diag(w_axis) ⊗ G_marg`. Under it the sandwich equals π\* in exact arithmetic; without it the sandwich is the Hilbert adjoint and π\* is not.
- **Branch 1.** The continuous reference, in SymPy, on the continuous sphere S² with the measure `dΩ = dμ dφ`; its mass `∫ dΩ = 4π` is computed by SymPy from the measure, never typed (`algebra-of-record`).

##### The decisions, with their reasons

- **Where the types live: `orpheus/numerics`, with SymPy imported inside the functions that need it.** Both are physics-free (a region is an index into a partition, a group an index into an axis, and the S² measure is mathematics), which is the 2026-09-29 criterion for the question values; the census found no layer gate forbidding a third-party import in `numerics`, and `numerics/manifold.py` is the precedent for lazy SymPy. S6.21 asserts it.
- **The `srepr` stability defence: the SymPy version as a content part, with a RECORD pin beside it.** The two failure directions differ in kind. A new SymPy that writes a different `srepr` for the same expression gives a different key: a miss, which costs a recomputation. A new SymPy that PARSES a stored string into a different expression gives the same key for a different function: a wrong hit, which returns a wrong answer. A RECORD pin alone detects both only when the suite runs under the new version; a cache read by a user who upgraded SymPy and ran nothing is unprotected against the wrong hit. The version part forecloses the wrong hit at every read, at the price of a miss on every SymPy upgrade (the whole `Symbolic` cache is recomputed). The pin stays, as the early notice that the format moved. The full version string is recommended over major.minor, because nothing measured bounds a patch release's effect on `srepr`.
- **The fixed assumptions are `real=True`** (orchestrator ruling); not `nonnegative` for `r`, because a slab admits any r₀.
- **The sphere declares no azimuth reference.** A reference perpendicular to ê_r chosen at every position would be a nowhere-zero tangent field on the sphere, which does not exist; any choice is singular somewhere, and the 1-D sphere cannot observe the azimuth anyway. The consequence for P4/step 8 (a `Symbolic` that depends on φ, paired with a sphere, has no well-defined value) is `NEEDS` 3.

#### The step-8 rulings (2026-10-02; the test-architect re-specifies the table above against them)

The census (`scratch/reference_architecture/p1step8/census.md`, `[M]` at `11a3f058`): `Materials` is `{id: Mixture}` with macroscopic channels only (no nuclide survives `compute_macro_xs`); every method's F is the fission emission χ⊗νΣ_f alone, with (n,2n) in A; `SigT` is a stored copy, so scaling a removal channel would not move the collision operator before posing unit 3; of the 53 Sood/Atalay references, 22 ask k∞ with no geometry and 31 a critical extent; the Galerkin generators ask c; no reference asks a composition search.

**The user's rulings (2026-10-02):**
1. **The key is self-describing.** A question's parameter (and each point key) IS the coordinate value: `Eigen(CellCoefficient.every(FISSION_EMISSION))` for k, `Eigen(GeometryExtent(interval=0))` for a critical extent. Numerics still sees an opaque hashable key. The specification RESOLVES a key by checking the coordinate against its materials and geometry; there is no name-to-coordinate table, so nothing is defined twice and nothing is declared implicitly.
2. **A cell is `(material id, Channel)`, with `Channel` a closed enum of the three EMISSION channels**: fission emission (χ⊗νΣ_f), scattering emission (Σ_s), (n,2n) emission (Σ_2n). These are the only cells whose scaling today's operators realise; k is every fission-emission cell, c every emission cell. Removal channels (an absorber search) arrive with posing unit 3, which un-stores Σ_t.
3. **`GeometryExtent(interval=i)` is the width of interval i, in cm, with every interval outside it translated outward.** On a bare body it is the outer size; on a reflected slab it is the core under a fixed reflector. It covers the 31 extent references.
4. **`NuclideDensity` is deferred.** Its trigger: `Materials` keeping number densities. Step 8 resolves `CellCoefficient | GeometryExtent` only, and S8.1's boron leg goes with the deferral.

**The orchestrator's defaults (2026-10-02):**
5. **"A material carries a cell" means the cell is non-zero.** Every channel field exists on every `Mixture`, so a field-exists predicate refuses nothing. A `CellCoefficient` whose cells are zero in every material it names is a zero direction, refused at resolution (S8.1 (h4)).
6. **The chart zero, the physical value and the admissible range of a coordinate wait for #529**, which owns the mode law that reads them. Step 8 resolves and validates keys only.
7. **The group-count rule reads every material of the specification.** A specification is one energy discretisation, so a declared material with another group count is malformed whether or not the geometry uses it. Rules (b) and (c) live once in the input layer (`orpheus/data`), `MaterialMesh` calls them, and `InconsistentMaterialsError` moves to `orpheus/data` with its importers re-pointed and no shim (the census found that `MaterialMesh` already checks every declared material, contrary to its docstring; the move makes that the stated rule).
8. **Homes.** `Channel` and `CellCoefficient` live in `orpheus/data` (they name `Mixture`'s channels); `GeometryExtent` in `orpheus/geometry`; `Specification`, which composes materials, geometry and a question and is neither data nor geometry, in a new input-tier package `orpheus/specification` that may import `data`, `geometry` and `numerics` and nothing above, registered in `tests/gates/test_layer_imports.py`. The test-architect may propose another home if the layer table argues for one.
